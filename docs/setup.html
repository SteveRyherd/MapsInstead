Set up in about a minute

Safari extensions are off until you turn them on. These are the exact taps.

🛡️

Safari will show a warning during setup. It shows the same one for every extension, and it's normal. What it means, and why it's OK to allow.

Open Settings, then Apps

On iOS 17 and earlier, skip this step: Safari is directly in the main Settings list.

Open a Google page in Safari, then Manage Extensions

Tap “Maps Instead” in the same menu

Open the menu again. “Maps Instead” is now listed in it, just above Describe Extension.

Choose Always Allow…

"Allow for One Day" would mean setting it up again tomorrow. Safari's mention of passwords and credit cards is its standard wording, not something Maps Instead does. Here's what it actually does.

8

Choose Always Allow on Every Website

This sounds bigger than it is. Maps Instead only asks for Google's domains and Google's short-link domains, so “every website” here means every Google site. It still can't touch anything else. Safari treats each Google site separately, and this option covers them all at once.

Open the Maps Instead app once

That registers the extension with Safari. You can quit the app afterwards; the extension lives in Safari.

Safari, then Settings…, then Extensions

Tick “Maps Instead” in the list on the left. The app's Open Safari Extensions Settings button takes you straight here.

Allow it on Google sites

With “Maps Instead” selected, click Edit Websites…, then set For other websites to Allow. Maps Instead only asks for Google's domains, so this covers every Google site and nothing else. Why Safari's warning sounds worse than this.

Is it safe to allow this?

Yes. A Safari extension is a small add-on that changes how Safari behaves on certain websites. Safari can't know what any one extension will do, so its warning describes the most an extension could do on the sites it's allowed on. Every extension that works with web pages gets the same wording.

What Maps Instead does

  • Runs only on Google's sites, and looks only for links to Google Maps
  • Turns those links into Apple Maps links, on your device
  • Asks Google where a shortened map link leads, when the link doesn't say
  • Remembers two things: whether it's switched on, and how many links it has redirected

What it doesn't do

  • Run on any site outside Google, such as your bank
  • Read passwords, card numbers or what you type
  • Keep or send your browsing history
  • Use analytics, ads, accounts or servers of its own

You don't have to take our word for it

🍎

Apple reviews it

Maps Instead is sold only through the App Store, and Apple checks each version before release. Its privacy label there is "Data Not Collected".

🧱

Safari enforces the limits

The list of sites an extension can reach is fixed in the extension itself. Safari blocks it from everything else, whichever option you chose.

🔍

The code is public

Anyone can read exactly what it does on GitHub, including the list of sites it asks for.

Changed your mind?

You stay in charge. Switch it off in the same Settings screen, or delete the app and the extension goes with it. Nothing is left behind, because nothing was kept anywhere else. For any other extension, the same habits serve you well: get it from the App Store, check its privacy label, and notice which sites it asks for.

Not working?

The support page covers the three things that account for nearly every problem, and how to report a link that misbehaves.
